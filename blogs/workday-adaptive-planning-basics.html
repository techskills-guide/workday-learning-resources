Workday Adaptive Planning: A Beginner's Overview

An introduction to planning, budgeting and forecasting concepts in a connected business environment.

Home / Topics / Workday Adaptive Planning: A Beginner's Overview

Workday Adaptive Planning supports planning and analysis activities such as budgeting, forecasting, modelling and reporting. How an organization uses the platform depends on its planning processes, model design and configuration. For beginners, start with planning fundamentals before exploring individual features.

Core concepts

Budgeting and forecasting

A budget represents a planned financial position for a period, while a forecast is updated as assumptions and actual results change. Learn how teams distinguish approved budgets, working forecasts and actuals.

Dimensions and model structure

Planning models organize information using elements such as accounts, time periods, versions, departments or other business dimensions. Understand what each dimension represents and how it supports analysis.

Drivers and assumptions

Driver-based planning connects an output to operational assumptions. For example, a staffing forecast might use headcount, average compensation and expected hiring dates. Document assumptions clearly and test how changing them affects results.

Scenarios and reporting

Scenario analysis helps teams compare possible outcomes. Reports and dashboards communicate the assumptions and results, so readers can understand what changed and why.

A beginner exercise

  1. Choose a simple example such as a departmental annual budget.
  2. Identify time periods, accounts and business dimensions.
  3. Write down the assumptions that drive costs or revenue.
  4. Compare a base case with a high and low scenario.
  5. Explain the key differences in a short summary.
Use synthetic data in practice exercises. Actual planning models may contain confidential financial or workforce information.

Who may find this area useful?

Finance professionals, financial analysts, business partners and planning specialists may find these concepts relevant. Spreadsheet modelling and financial analysis experience can be helpful, but role requirements vary.
