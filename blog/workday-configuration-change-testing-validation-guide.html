Workday Training: Practising Configuration Changes, Testing and Validation

A practical guide to understanding change impact, testing business scenarios and reviewing configuration outcomes in an authorised learning environment.

Learning Workday involves more than understanding menus, business processes and reports. A learner should also understand how configuration changes are planned, tested and reviewed before they become part of a live business environment.

Imagine an organisation wants to update an approval condition in an employee business process. The change may look small, but it could affect employee groups, approval responsibilities or downstream activities. Making the change without checking its impact can create unexpected problems.

This is why configuration practice and testing are important parts of Workday learning. They help learners understand not only what a setting does, but also how to evaluate its effect on a business process.

Practice safely: Use only an authorised training or non-production tenant for exercises. Never experiment in a live customer environment or use real employee and confidential financial data in learning examples.

Why configuration practice matters

Workday is configured to support an organisation's business requirements. Different organisations may use different approval rules, security arrangements, reporting structures and operational processes. Practical exercises help learners connect system behaviour with the business reason behind a configuration.

  • Understand the business requirement behind a proposed change.
  • Identify which process, role or data area may be affected.
  • Check whether the expected result occurs after the change.
  • Explain the result clearly and document any issue found during testing.

The objective is not to make random changes. It is to follow a structured approach and understand the reasoning behind each decision.

Understand Workday tenants before making changes

A tenant is a separate Workday instance. Organisations may use different tenants for live operations, testing, implementation activities and release preparation. The names and availability of environments depend on the organisation's Workday arrangements.

Production tenant

The live environment used for actual business operations and organisational data. It is not an environment for unauthorised practice.

Sandbox tenant

A non-production environment that may be used to test configurations, features and changes before they are considered for production.

Sandbox Preview tenant

An environment that can help organisations review upcoming functionality and understand how planned releases may affect existing processes.

Implementation tenant

An environment that can support configuration, prototyping, testing and implementation activities during a project.

Do not assume every learner has access to all tenant types. Follow the access rules and guidance provided by the organisation or training provider.

Practical exercise: review an approval configuration

Consider a fictional organisation where employee information changes require manager approval. The organisation wants to introduce an additional review step for selected employee changes. A learner should approach this requirement systematically instead of immediately changing a setting.

Step 1: Understand the business requirement

First, identify what the organisation wants to achieve. Which employee changes require the additional review? Should the rule apply to everyone or only a selected group? Who should perform the review? What should happen if the condition is not met?

Step 2: Identify the relevant process

Identify the business process associated with the requirement. Understand its existing steps, participants, approval conditions and notifications. Review the current process before suggesting any changes. This helps prevent modifying the wrong area or overlooking an existing dependency.

Step 3: Assess the possible impact

A configuration change may affect more than one scenario. Consider employee groups covered by the process, manager responsibilities, approval routing, process completion time, notifications and exception handling. Write down the expected impact before testing the proposed change.

Step 4: Prepare test scenarios

Testing should cover more than the expected successful outcome. For the fictional approval requirement, prepare positive, negative and exception-based scenarios.

Test scenarioExpected result to verify
Employee change meets the conditionAdditional review is triggered as designed.
Employee change does not meet the conditionExisting process continues according to its configured path.
Required approver is unavailableThe configured alternate or exception path is followed, if one exists.
Transaction contains incomplete informationThe applicable validation or correction process is followed.

The actual outcome depends on the tenant's configuration. The purpose of the exercise is to compare expected behaviour with observed results, not to assume every tenant works in the same way.

Step 5: Record and review results

For each test, record the requirement, configuration area reviewed, test scenario, expected outcome, actual outcome, issue identified and action required. A clear testing record makes it easier for another team member to understand what was checked.

How to validate a configuration change

Validation is not simply checking whether a configuration task was completed. A proper review considers the complete business requirement.

  • Functional validation: Does the process behave according to the intended business rule?
  • Role validation: Are the correct participants and approvers involved?
  • Data validation: Does the process work with the relevant employee or transaction information?
  • Exception validation: What happens when normal conditions are not met?
  • Regression testing: Does the change affect an existing process that was previously working?

For larger changes, testing may also involve end-to-end scenarios and user acceptance testing. The testing approach should match the scope and risk of the change.

Understand configuration change tracking

Implementation teams also need to understand how configuration changes are documented and moved between environments. Workday provides tools such as Configuration Change Tracker to help identify configuration changes made by selected users during a specified period. This information can support preparation and review of configuration packages for migration.

Identifying a change does not automatically guarantee that every dependency has been included. Teams still need to review the package, check prerequisites and validate the result in the target environment. This is an important concept because configuration work involves both making changes and managing them responsibly.

Common mistakes learners should avoid

Changing settings without understanding the requirement

A configuration setting should solve a defined business problem, not simply be changed for practice.

Testing only one scenario

A successful test does not prove that other employee groups or process conditions will behave correctly.

Ignoring existing configurations

New rules may interact with existing approvals, security roles or process conditions.

Skipping documentation

Without a clear record, it becomes difficult to understand what was changed, why it was changed and what was tested.

Assuming sandbox results guarantee production behaviour

Different environments may have differences in data, configuration or access. Proper review and testing are still necessary before deployment.

Build a practical Workday learning routine

  1. Select one business requirement, such as an employee information change.
  2. Identify the Workday process connected with that requirement.
  3. Study the relevant configuration concepts.
  4. Prepare positive, negative and exception-based test scenarios.
  5. Review expected and actual results in an authorised learning environment.
  6. Document observations and explain why a particular result occurred.

Repeating this method across HCM, Finance and other relevant functional areas can help learners develop a stronger understanding of Workday implementation activities.

What to look for in Workday training

When comparing learning programmes, look beyond the number of topics listed in a syllabus. Check whether the learning approach explains business requirements, configuration concepts, testing scenarios and practical problem-solving. A structured workday training programme should help learners connect functional concepts with practical exercises and understand how different Workday components support business operations.

It is also useful to ask whether the programme includes trainer-led explanations, scenario discussions, practice activities and feedback on common mistakes.

Final thoughts

Understanding configuration changes is an important step in moving from basic Workday familiarity towards practical implementation knowledge. The most useful learning approach combines business requirement analysis, configuration awareness, scenario-based testing and clear documentation. These habits help learners understand why a change is needed, how it should behave and what must be checked before it is approved.
